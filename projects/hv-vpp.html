Controls & Mechanical Design - 01 / 06

HV‑VPP Resin Printer

I built a fully custom high-viscosity vat photopolymerization (HV-VPP) 3D printer, enabling the creation of silicone actuators for eventual use in soft robotics.

Lab
Digital Design & Manufacturing Lab
Duration
Summer 2026
Role
Controls + Mechanical

HV-VPP stands for high-viscosity vat photopolymerization - it uses UV light masked through an LCD panel to cure liquid resin layer by layer. While standard resin printers rely on thin, low-viscosity resins, this machine processes dense, high-viscosity materials instead, requiring a custom approach to layer recoating and leveling.

I joined the project when it was just a printer frame and turned it into a functional prototype. My work focused on two main areas: architecting the PLC control system and engineering key mechanical hardware.

Bringing The Controls Online

The printer runs on a Beckhoff PLC programmed in TwinCAT. I reconfigured the system to run as an EtherCAT master, allowing the Beckhoff controller to drive all motors and actuators directly.

Z-axis running under PLC control during movement and repeatability testing.

Building The Mechanical System

To ensure optical stability, the breadboard frame was locked to the outer chassis using custom standoffs rather than adjustable feet. I validated the design using FEA under a 400 lbf static and buckling load. To avoid the warping seen in early SLA prototypes during UV curing, I produced the final standoffs out of SLS nylon and finish-machined them for surface flatness.

Additionally, I turned custom servo and roller couplers out of 303 stainless steel, maintaining a press fit for clamping. I also fabricated custom dovetail gauge blocks to align the recoating rollers in-plane while allowing quick removal during maintenance.

UV & LCD Masking

Each UV power supply dims via a 0–10V analog signal. By integrating an EL4001 bus terminal, the PLC can directly manage power output across all ten supplies simultaneously. Layer masking is driven through LCD panels controlled by a custom Python slicer script.

PLC-driven UV light controls running via the EL4001 terminal.

Tramming & First Prints

Initial alignment showed the build plate was approximately 1° out of plane from the breadboard. Precise shimming at the standoffs corrected the offset and aligned the plate parallel to the resin vat.

During initial test runs with 0.5 mm layer heights and 1-minute exposure times, the printer successfully cured three-layer test structures measuring 1.5 mm in thickness. Future iterations will focus on improving layer-to-layer adhesion, refining load distribution across the build plate, and adjusting the Z-axis homing logic.

Built alongside Roxy Carbonell, Matt McCoy, and Keenan Vaughn under Dr. Carolyn Seepersad at the Digital Design & Manufacturing Lab.
